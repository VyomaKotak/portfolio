03 — How I think

Three habits from four years on call.

Production systems engineering leaves marks. These are the ones that shape how I build machine learning.

🛡️

Design for failure first

My first question isn't "which algorithm" but "what breaks this in production?" Class imbalance, schema drift, train/serve skew: I plan for them before the metric.

📡

Reliability is a feature

A model that can't score in real time is a research paper, not a product. Monitoring, containerisation and reproducibility come standard, not as an afterthought.

💬

Translate, don't just compute

Data science that can't be communicated is just expensive maths. I turn technical findings into insight both technical and business audiences can act on.

Real-Time Fraud Detection Platform

Flagship · Streaming ML
GitHub ↗
PythonKafkaSpark Structured StreamingFastAPIStreamlitDocker
Problem
Fraud is only 0.13% of transactions. A model that predicts "never fraud" is 99.87% accurate and completely worthless. Transactions must be judged in milliseconds, at thousands per second, without blocking real customers.
Approach
An end-to-end streaming platform scoring transactions through Kafka, Spark Structured Streaming, an ML model, FastAPI and a live dashboard. A shared feature module runs across training, the API and the Spark scorer to kill train/serve skew, with the decision threshold exposed for the business to tune against a cost matrix.
Why it's different
It treats the imbalance honestly, reporting PR-AUC rather than the flattering ROC-AUC, and treats the false-positive tradeoff as a business decision, not a hidden default.

Amazon Review Sentiment at Scale

Flagship · Big Data NLP
GitHub ↗
PythonPySparkSpark MLlibTF-IDFSHAPTableau
Problem
43.9M Amazon Electronics reviews (21 GB) of unread feedback. At this scale pandas runs out of memory before it finishes loading the file.
Approach
A six-stage PySpark ML pipeline (Tokenizer, StopWords, TF-IDF, Scaler) benchmarking four classifiers with cross-validation. Optimised with caching, repartitioning and skew analysis, explained with SHAP, delivered as four Tableau dashboards.
Why it's different
The finding earns its keep: a well-tuned linear model beat tree ensembles on high-dimensional sparse text, where ensembles struggle to split across 10,000 features.
